# Branch stock and replenishment flow

- Cashiers may manually add positive stock to their own branch only when both the global master and that branch's input permission are enabled. Use the existing persisted branch permission for independent locks; when locked, the UI and API both reject cashier intake. Cashiers must not adjust another branch's stock or perform stock-out/opname through this permission.
- Keep Owner-set purchase quantities per product and branch as the source of truth. Branch-specific sales history may suggest quantities, but Owner reviews and decides the final amounts.
- Target coverage days are optional. If blank, still show products in the selected categories that are visible to selected branches, with current stock and empty manual quantity fields; hide only suggested quantities. If set to a positive whole number, show editable recommendations.
  **Why:** The Owner clarified that omitting days should hide suggested buy quantities, not hide the eligible product list.
  **How to apply:** Skip sales-summary requests when blank; populate the manual product list and branch stock instead. When positive whole days are entered, calculate suggestions for the selected categories and branches, keeping all final quantities Owner-editable.
- Source the Owner's purchase candidates from products already in the automatic branch shopping basket, not the full active catalog. Target days change the suggested quantity, not whether those basket products remain visible.
  **Why:** The Owner wants the purchase list to contain products actually flagged by the branch's minimum-stock rule, rather than overwhelm the screen with up to 1,000 active products.
  **How to apply:** Filter candidates by selected branch, category, and basket membership; with no target days show current stock and blank quantities, and with a target show editable suggestions. Only add products outside the basket through manual search if the Owner explicitly chooses that behavior.
- When the Owner saves a purchase plan, a blank branch quantity means the product is not being bought for that branch; any entered quantity must be at least 1. Publish the plan directly to each branch cashier. The planned quantity is the default receipt: if all items match, the cashier confirms without entering actual quantities and the system records the planned amount as received. For exceptions, the cashier chooses SHORTAGE and enters how many pieces are missing, NOT_AVAILABLE for a whole missing line, or EXTRA and enters how many additional pieces arrived; every exception requires a reason. The API derives received quantity (planned minus shortage, zero, or planned plus extra) and adds only that quantity to stock. Owner receipt details show planned, received, difference, reason, cashier, and confirmation time. A confirmation closes the branch receipt even with shortages, missing items, or extras; do not leave a remainder pending. The Owner handles supplier complaints outside the app and decides any new purchase separately. Once a branch confirms, lock that branch's receipt; use a new plan for additional purchases.
  **Why:** Cashiers should verify what arrived against the Owner's note without retyping quantities, while every stock discrepancy remains explicit and auditable.
  **How to apply:** Keep normal confirmation quantity-free; require a reason for shortage, unavailable, and extra cases; validate derived quantities server-side and use them for stock changes and Owner summaries.
- After publication, Owner edits create a revision, preserve the prior version, and notify affected branches. Confirmed branches remain locked and must not be changed by a later revision.
- Owner may permanently delete a draft or published plan only while every branch receipt is still unconfirmed and open; deletion removes the plan's receipts and revision history and never changes stock.
- New transfer-request creation is retired. Preserve and allow resolution of pending legacy requests so existing records are not stranded; current cashier transfers are sender-initiated, grouped multi-product shipments, with no Owner approval step.
- Do not add automatic stock-transfer suggestions to the current plan or candidate; keep the existing manual sender, recipient-confirmation, history, and return flows unchanged.
- A transfer approval is not a stock movement. Reduce source available stock when the actual shipment is dispatched, track it in transit, and increase destination stock only for the quantity that receiving staff confirms. Pre-dispatch cancellations release the pending amount; post-dispatch returns only restore source stock after physical return receipt.
- For nearby branches that hand stock over in person, receiving confirmation is a second-party acknowledgment that verifies the sender's recorded quantity and prevents unlogged transfers; discrepancy handling is an exception path, not the primary purpose of confirmation.
- A wrong SKU recorded at dispatch is not edited by the receiving branch. The receiver rejects and returns the entire original line; it adds no stock at the destination. The sender restores the original recorded SKU only after confirming the physical return, then creates a new transfer using the correct SKU.
- For a wrong-SKU transfer record, do not let the receiver revise the product or quantity. Return the whole line to the sender; the source cashier confirms the physical return before the original recorded SKU is restored. The sender then starts a fresh transfer with the correct SKU. Keep the original transfer and linked return history; do not credit the receiver for the rejected line.
- Design the whole purchasing/transfer flow for the VPS's limited resources and mobile use: bounded database aggregation, no loading unbounded transaction history into API memory, responsive screens, and monitoring of database load, RAM, and response time.
- Preserve auditable purchase, transfer, dispatch, receipt, and discrepancy history. If detailed sales are archived, retain compact per-branch/product sales summaries needed for future restock recommendations; do not silently delete records needed for reconciliation.
- Recommended resource-safe forecast design: keep a separate rolling 30-day daily-quantity summary keyed by branch/product/day, update it incrementally in small background batches with idempotent upserts, and serve cached summaries only to Owner purchasing screens. Do not run full-history forecasting on cashier requests.
- Never prune or rewrite `DailyIncomeSummary` or `Commission` as part of product-demand summary cleanup. Their financial/bonus retention is independent and must remain intact.

**Why:** the Owner asked to keep manual branch-level purchasing decisions, control cashier intake per branch, let cashiers transfer without Owner approval, and require confirmation at each physical handoff. The Owner clarified that saving a plan publishes per-branch product quantities to cashiers as goods being purchased; later edits must be identifiable revisions and notify affected branches. At receipt, cashiers record actual quantities, mark absent products, and notify the Owner of shortages, with only the quantity actually received entering stock. The Owner chose to close the receipt as complete at the actual quantity, with an explicit shortage state; unreceived quantity is not kept pending and supplier complaints stay outside the app. The Owner later clarified that a branch receipt becomes locked after cashier confirmation; any further purchase is a new plan, preventing a revision from crediting already-received stock twice. The Owner explicitly chose permanent deletion of an erroneous plan before any branch confirms, accepting that its revisions and pending receipts are removed. The Owner explicitly excluded automatic transfer suggestions from the current plan and candidate. For a wrong SKU recorded by the sender, the Owner chose a simple whole-line return and re-send flow instead of in-app SKU correction; the sender must confirm the item physically came back before the stock ledger is restored. For ordinary quantity differences, record only the actual quantity and discuss the reason outside the app.

**How to apply:** enforce both permission levels on the server as well as in the UI; treat purchase calculations as recommendations only, do not add transfer recommendations without a new explicit scope decision, preserve the actual invoice/shipment/receipt trail, and never post stock before receipt confirmation. Keep Owner purchase receipts branch-scoped, preserve every published revision and notify affected branches, preserve planned versus actual quantities, inform the Owner about shortages/absent items, close the cashier's inspection as complete with a visible shortage status, lock a confirmed branch receipt, and keep it distinct from unrelated manual cashier stock intake. For Owner-requested permanent deletion, allow only drafts or plans whose receipts remain open and unconfirmed; block if any branch has confirmed or closed a receipt, delete the associated pending receipt and revision rows together, and do not change stock.
